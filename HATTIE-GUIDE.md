# Looking after your website

This is for you, Hattie. No technical knowledge assumed. Nothing in here can
break the site — if something looks wrong, close the tab and it will be exactly
as it was.

---

## Getting in

1. Go to **hattieshighlights.com/settings**
2. Type your password.

That's it — no account to create, no email to verify, no username. One password.

**Bookmark that page now.** It's the one thing worth doing before you read the
rest.

There's also a small ⚙ symbol at the bottom of the menu on every page of your
site. That's the same door. Visitors won't know what it is, and clicking it just
shows them a password box.

You stay signed in for about eight hours, then it asks again. If you're on a
shared or borrowed computer, click **Sign out** when you're done.

> **Forgotten the password?** It genuinely cannot be looked up — it isn't stored
> anywhere, only a scrambled version of it is. Ask whoever set this up and they
> will issue you a new one in about a minute.

## What you'll see

Four tabs across the top. That's the whole thing:

| Tab | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Photos** | Add, describe, reorder, star, and delete |
| **Home page** | The big photo and the words on it |
| **About page** | Your photo and your story |
| **Banner** | The strip across the top |

Nothing else is editable from here, on purpose. Prices, categories, and the
booking link change rarely enough that it's better to ask than to have thirty
fields staring at you every time you want to swap a photo.

## Adding photos

On the **Photos** tab:

1. Tap **Choose Files** and pick as many as you like at once.
2. Choose a **Category**.
3. Write a **description** (see below — this one matters).
4. Tap **Add photos**.

They appear in the list straight away, marked **New** — they go live when you
publish.

### Drag them straight off the camera

Don't resize anything first. Full-size exports are what you want — the site
makes its own smaller copies automatically and sends the right size to each
visitor's screen.

**Your camera records where each photo was taken.** That's a real privacy
problem when the photo is of somebody's family. Every photo you upload has that
stripped out automatically before it is saved — nothing with a location on it is
ever stored or published. You don't have to do anything, and it doesn't touch
the picture quality at all.

If a photo is refused, the reason will say why — usually it's not a photo file
(a PDF or a screenshot saved oddly), or it's enormous. Nothing breaks.

### "Describe this photo"

This is the one field worth slowing down for. Write what's happening, like
you're describing it to someone on the phone:

> ✅ "Bride and her dad laughing on the church steps"
>
> ❌ "photo1" or "wedding"

Two reasons. People who can't see the photo — using a screen reader — hear this
instead. And it's a big part of how Google works out what your photos are of, so
it's some of the most valuable typing you'll ever do for getting found.

## Changing the order photos appear

Use the **↑** and **↓** buttons on any photo. That's the order they appear on
the site, and it saves as soon as you tap.

(Buttons rather than dragging, deliberately — dragging a photo up a long list on
a phone is fiddly and easy to get wrong.)

---

## Choosing what's on the home page

Tap **☆ Add to home page** on any photo. Tap it again to take it off.

That's the whole system. The home page grid is simply the photos you've starred,
in the order you've put them in. There's no second list to keep in step.

Six or so works best. More than that and the page gets long.

## The availability banner

On the **Banner** tab:

- **Show it on every page** — on or off
- **What it says** — e.g. *"Booking fall 2026 — 3 dates left"*

When it's on, it appears across the top of every page. When it's off, it's gone
completely.

This is the single most useful thing on the site to keep current. It's the
difference between someone assuming you're probably booked and someone getting
in touch. Update it when your availability changes and leave it off when it
isn't true — an out-of-date banner is worse than none.

## Changing the words

- **The home page headline** → *Home page* tab. It's in three parts because one
  word is set in the fancy handwriting font — that box shows the font as you
  type. Keep it to one word. It's beautiful and very hard to read in long runs.
- **Your About text** → *About page* tab. One big box; leave a blank line
  between paragraphs. The text currently starts with "TODO: Hattie to replace" —
  that's a note to you. Replace it and delete the note.
- **Swapping the big photo or your portrait** → same tabs, at the top. Choose a
  file, describe it, tap **Swap photo**.

Prices and categories aren't editable here. Ask and they'll be changed for you.

## Publishing

Nothing you change goes live straight away. Everything you do is held as a
draft, and a bar at the bottom of the screen tells you whether there's anything
waiting:

> **Unpublished changes · 2 new photos**

When you're happy, tap **Publish changes**. Then give it a minute or two — the
site rebuilds itself and your changes appear.

Two things worth knowing:

- **Your draft is saved as you go.** Close the tab, come back tomorrow, and
  everything you'd done is still waiting. Nothing is lost.
- **Discard throws the draft away** and puts everything back to how the site
  currently looks. It only affects unpublished changes.

If you remove a photo by mistake and have already published, it can still be
recovered — every publish is saved permanently. Just ask.

## Getting found on Google

**Do this once, and do it before anything else.** It costs nothing and it is by
a wide margin the highest-return thing available to you:

### Claim your Google Business Profile

1. Go to **google.com/business**
2. Sign in and search for your business name.
3. Claim it (or create it), and set the website to **hattieshighlights.com**.
4. Fill in your city, service area, and hours.
5. Add ten of your best photos.

This is what puts you on Google Maps and in the little box on the right of a
search. For a photographer, "senior photos [your city]" is worth more than any
amount of general traffic, and this profile is most of how you win it.

### Then

Ask happy clients to leave a Google review. Nothing else moves the needle like
that — a handful of recent reviews beats almost anything else you could do.

## If something goes wrong

**A photo won't upload.** Read the message — it says exactly which file and
why. Usually it isn't actually a photo (a PDF or an odd screenshot), or it's
enormous. The other photos in the same batch still go through.

**I changed something and the site looks the same.** Did you tap **Publish
changes**? If you did, give it two minutes and reload — the site takes a moment
to rebuild.

**It asked for my password again.** Normal — it does that every eight hours.

**I deleted something by accident.** It's recoverable. Don't try to fix it
yourself — ask, and mention roughly when it happened.

**The booking calendar is blank.** Nothing you can fix from here — ask. In the
meantime people can still email you: that link sits right underneath the
calendar and always works.
